# Testing & Scaling — Applied to the 6 Digital Products

Same discipline as the AI T-Shirt Playbook's Chapter 7, applied to CAD + UK's 3 products each. The mindset: you cannot predict which of the 6 sells — you can only test for it. Ship, measure, double down on the winner, retire the rest without sentiment.

## The loop

1. **Analyze** — after the first 1–2 weeks live, compare views, opt-ins (free lead magnets), and actual purchases per product. Even small numbers show a relative winner.
2. **Improve** — take whichever product is ahead and iterate: a second colourway/season for the Wall Art Bundle, a premium tier for the Organization Suite or Colour Kit, an adjacent bundle.
3. **Scale** — push the winner to more articles, more CTAs, and (once there's traffic to justify it) paid promotion. Do not spend more build time on the other 5 until one has real numbers.

## Applied specifically here

- **Don't launch all 6 at once.** Launch 1 per country first (recommended: Organization Suite for CAD, Rented Flat Makeover Kit for UK — both have the strongest article clusters), and let the other 2 per country sit built-but-unpublished.
- **The playbook's conversion benchmark** (a design converting at 6.4% vs. 1.2–2.8% for the rest is the signal to double down, not a marginal edge) applies directly: if one product's opt-in or CTA click-through is several times the others, that's your answer — stop guessing and build around it.
- **Sunk-cost warning carries over directly:** the product you like best (or worked hardest on — almost certainly the Wall Art Bundle, since it's the slow one) and the product that actually sells are often different products. Follow the data.

## Cadence

- **Weekly:** once live, check opt-in/click data per product.
- **Bi-weekly:** tag each of the 6 as keep / iterate / shelve based on real numbers, not effort spent.
- **Monthly:** take the strongest performer and build one expansion around it (second bundle, premium tier, seasonal variant) rather than starting a 7th unrelated product.
- **Quarterly:** revisit the 2 shelved products per country — a product that didn't work with the current traffic level may work once the blog's SEO position changes.
