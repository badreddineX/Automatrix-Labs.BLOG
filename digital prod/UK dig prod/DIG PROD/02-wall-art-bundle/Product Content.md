## BONUS PDF 1 — The Gallery Wall Layout Guide

*Four layouts, drawn to scale with spacing measurements.*

**Layout 1 — The Grid (4 prints, equal size)**
Two by two, 5 cm between frames. The most forgiving arrangement. Works above a sofa, a bed, or a console. With A4 frames the total footprint is roughly 47 × 65 cm.

**Layout 2 — The Chimney Breast Stack (2–3 prints)**
Vertical, 5 cm apart, centred on the chimney breast. This is the layout for a period property — most British living rooms have a chimney breast and most gallery wall guides ignore it entirely.

**Layout 3 — The Horizontal Run (3 prints)**
Three in a line at eye level, 8 cm apart. Best above a sofa or bed where the wall is wider than it is tall.

**Layout 4 — The Leaning Stack (2–3 prints, zero wall contact)**
Larger frames leaned on a shelf, mantelpiece, console, or the floor. **This is the renter's layout** — the same visual weight as a hung gallery wall with nothing touching the wall at all. A mantelpiece lean is the most naturally British version of this and needs no hardware whatsoever.

**Universal rule:** hang the centre of the arrangement at 145–150 cm from the floor. This is standard gallery eye level and the single most common mistake in home gallery walls — nearly everyone hangs too high.

## BONUS PDF 2 — The Deposit-Safe Hanging Guide

*How to hang all 12 prints in a UK rental without a single deduction.*

☐ **Adhesive picture-hanging strips** — the primary method. Check the pack's weight rating against your frame weight, apply to a clean wall, and remove by pulling the release tab slowly downward (from ~£6 per multipack, Amazon UK)

☐ **Lean, don't hang** — mantelpiece, shelf, console, or floor. Zero wall contact, and in a period room with a fireplace it reads as deliberate styling rather than compromise

☐ **Picture ledge on adhesive strips** — swap prints seasonally without touching the wall again

☐ **Washi tape borders** — frame the print directly on the wall with decorative tape. No frame cost at all, fully removable

☐ **Existing picture rail** — many Victorian and Edwardian properties still have one. Hanging from the rail with cord or chain requires no fixings at all and is the most period-correct option available. Check before you assume there isn't one

**Before applying any adhesive:** wipe the wall with rubbing alcohol and let it dry completely. Adhesive fails on dust and on paint that hasn't fully cured — and a failed strip takes paint with it.

**Frame sourcing in the UK:** IKEA is the cheapest reliable option across A-series sizes (A4 frames from ~£3.50). Dunelm and Wilko cover the mid-range. The Range and TK Maxx are worth checking for larger sizes at short notice. **Buy all frames in one colour** — mixed frame colours are what makes a gallery wall look accidental rather than curated.

## Printing Guide (include this in the listing description too)

| Print Method | Cost | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Home inkjet | Paper cost only | A5 and A4 — fastest, good on matte cardstock |
| Boots / Max Spielmann photo | ~£1–6 per print | A4 and smaller, reliable colour |
| Snappy Snaps / local print shop | £8–25 | A3 and up, best quality, worth it for one statement piece |
| Online print service | ~£3–15 | Bulk — cheapest per print if you're doing all 12 |

**Paper recommendation:** matte or lightly textured cardstock, 200 gsm or heavier. Glossy photo paper fights the muted palette these designs use, and glare is a real problem in British rooms lit by a single overhead fixture on a grey afternoon.

**For Set C specifically:** the dark designs need a print service rather than a home inkjet. Heavy ink coverage on consumer printers produces banding and colour shift. This is stated plainly here rather than left for a buyer to discover — managing that expectation up front prevents the most likely refund request on this product.

### Production notes (not part of the buyer-facing product)

- **This is the bottleneck product.** 12 designs × 5 sizes = 60 files, each needing a native layout rather than a stretched master. Budget the majority of the build window here
- **Generate the art, don't stock-source it.** Use the `design` / `banner-design` skills (Gemini image generation) with the palette locked across all 12. Stock sources carry licensing risk on a product being resold
- **Pull the palette from the blog's own colour research** — the living-room-colour-schemes and bedroom-colour-ideas articles already establish which tones work in UK light (warm greige, sage, muted terracotta, forest green, charcoal, brass). Using that same palette makes the art bundle and the Colour Kit visibly part of one catalogue
- **A-series sizing leads, imperial follows.** This is the inverse of the Canadian bundle and it matters — a UK buyer who opens a ZIP full of 8×10" files knows immediately the product wasn't made for them
- **JPG is primary, PDF is the bonus.** JPG at 300 DPI is what UK high-street print services and home printers handle most reliably, and it stays under the 20MB per-file cap
- **Never pure white backgrounds** — off-white and cream print cleanly on consumer printers; pure white exposes every calibration difference
- **Set C is the highest-risk set to produce.** Heavy ink coverage prints badly at home. Design it tonally rather than as flat dark blocks, and keep the printing-guide warning in the buyer-facing copy — do not quietly drop it to make the listing sound better
- **Differentiator:** Set C (dark and moody, built for period rooms) plus the picture-rail and chimney-breast guidance. Competitors sell print files; almost none of them account for the architecture of an actual British house. Lead the previews with the mantelpiece lean and the chimney-breast stack
- **Preview images:** mock the prints into a real British interior — a period room with a chimney breast, not a blank white wall. Buyers decide from previews before reading a word
- **Free lead-magnet extraction:** one print from Set A at A4 only, or the gallery wall layout guide on its own
