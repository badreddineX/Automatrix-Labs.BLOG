# The Rented Flat Makeover Kit
### Transform a UK Rental Without Risking a Penny of Your Deposit

*Built for britishhomeinterior.co.uk*

**Format:** PDF (A4, A5, US Letter) + editable budget worksheet
**Target price:** $15–19 / £12–15

## Why This Kit Exists

Around 4.6 million households in England rent privately — roughly 19% of all households (English Housing Survey 2023–24). Most of them are living in someone else's magnolia box, paying more rent than ever, and holding back on making the place feel like home because of one fear: losing the deposit.

This kit removes that fear. Every change in it is reversible. None of them require landlord permission except where explicitly noted, and the one that does comes with the exact wording to ask for it.

**A note on prices and product names:** every item lists a category first (what to look for) and a specific example second. Retailers discontinue and rename products constantly — treat the named products as a starting reference, not a shopping list to follow blindly.

## PAGE 2 — Start With the Light (The £20 Change That Does the Most)

Most British rentals come fitted with bare bulbs or 4000–6500K "cool white" / "daylight" bulbs. These make every room feel like a waiting room, and no amount of furniture fixes it.

☐ **Replace every bulb in the flat** — kitchen, bathroom, hallway, bedroom, living room — with **2700K warm white LEDs**. Full flat replacement typically costs £15–25 (4-packs from ~£8, Amazon UK)

☐ **Keep the original bulbs in a labelled bag.** Your tenancy requires the property returned as found. Bulbs back in on exit, no issue

☐ **Add a plug-in pendant light** where you're stuck with a bare overhead bulb. It hangs from a ceiling hook and runs a fabric cable to a plug socket — no electrician, no rewiring. From ~£22 (Dunelm), ~£25 (Next Home), ~£45 (John Lewis). One in the living room and one in the bedroom transforms both

☐ **Never rely on the overhead light alone.** Two or three lamps at different heights beats one ceiling fixture in every room, every time

**Why this is first:** it costs the least, takes an evening, and changes how the flat feels the moment you walk in — before you've bought a single piece of furniture.

## PAGE 3 — The Rug Rule

A correctly sized rug is the single most transformative deposit-safe purchase available. It covers dated or damaged flooring completely, defines zones in open-plan layouts, and adds warmth to a room that has none.

**The sizing rule is non-negotiable:**

☐ **Living room** — all front legs of the sofa and chairs must sit on the rug
☐ **Bedroom** — the rug extends at least 60 cm beyond the bed on both sides
☐ **Dining area** — chairs stay on the rug even when pulled out

**An undersized rug is worse than no rug.** It makes the furniture look like it's floating awkwardly, and it's the most common single mistake in rented flats.

**Where to buy:** jute-style 200×290 cm from ~£85 (Dunelm), 200×300 cm from ~£89 (IKEA STOENSE range), or ~£65 in Wayfair sales.

☐ **The zoning trick:** in an open-plan flat, a rug under the dining table defines the dining zone even though it shares a room with the living area. The eye reads the rug boundary as a room boundary. This costs nothing beyond the rug and is the most underused technique in UK rentals.

## PAGE 4 — Make Landlord Furniture Look Like Yours

Furnished rentals come with sofas and beds in beige, grey, or brown — not because landlords have no taste, but because neutral furniture is cheapest to re-let. The job is to cover it until it reads as yours.

**The sofa (about ten minutes, ~£45):**
☐ Two cushion covers in one colour (from ~£6 each, Dunelm)
☐ Two more in a complementary texture or pattern
☐ One throw over the armrest or back

**The bed (~£40):**
☐ A flat sheet folded back over the duvet
☐ Two euro square pillows, 65×65 cm, in your own pillowcases behind the sleeping pillows (from ~£12 each)
☐ A throw folded at the foot

The duvet underneath can be the landlord's. Nobody can tell.

**Curtains:**
☐ Replace the landlord's curtains using the existing pole or track — permitted in almost all tenancies, and you take yours with you. From ~£22 per pair (Dunelm), blackout lining from ~£35
☐ **Hang them as high as possible** — just below the ceiling, not just above the window frame. This is the single most common proportion mistake in rented flats and it costs nothing to get right
☐ For a recess with no existing fitting, an extendable tension rod works with no drilling. Check the weight rating for windows over 100 cm wide

## PAGE 5 — The Deposit-Safe Gallery Wall

A gallery wall does more to make a rented space feel personally yours than almost any other single change, and it's fully reversible.

**What you need:**
☐ Adhesive picture-hanging strips — check the pack's weight rating against your frame weight, and remove by pulling the release tab slowly downward (from ~£6 per multipack, Amazon UK)
☐ Frames in two or three sizes, **all the same colour** — all white or all black, never mixed (A4 frames from ~£3.50, IKEA RIBBA range)
☐ Content: personal photographs, printed-at-home art, or free public-domain downloads from museum open collections

**The method:**
☐ Lay the entire arrangement on the floor first and photograph it before you mount anything
☐ Leave 5–8 cm between frames
☐ Group small frames together rather than scattering them
☐ Hang the centre of the whole arrangement at 145–150 cm from the floor — standard gallery eye level, and most people hang too high

**Before applying any adhesive:** wipe the wall with rubbing alcohol and let it dry fully. Adhesive fails on dust and on paint that hasn't cured — and a failed strip takes paint with it, which is the exact outcome you're avoiding.

☐ A large leaner mirror propped against the wall, no fixings at all, from ~£30 (Dunelm)
☐ Position it opposite the main window to bounce daylight around the room
☐ Arched and rounded shapes from ~£35–65 (Next Home, H&M Home) if you want it to read as decor rather than utility

**Hardware swap — 20 minutes, fully reversible:**
☐ Replace builder-standard chrome cabinet handles with brushed brass, matte black, or antique bronze (£2–4 each, B&Q, or a coordinated set of 10 from ~£18)
☐ Same applies to interior door handles (~£6–8 each)
☐ **Keep every original in a labelled bag** and reinstall on exit

**Plants and scent — the finishing layer:**
☐ Low-light survivors for a typical rental: snake plant, pothos, ZZ plant (£5–12 each)
☐ Good faux for dark flats or frequent travellers — large faux monstera ~£20, fiddle leaf fig ~£25 (IKEA FEJKA range)
☐ Reed diffuser from ~£5 (Dunelm), soy candle from ~£8 (Next Home). Pick one or two scents and use them consistently throughout — consistency matters more than which scent

## PAGE 7 — Know Your Deposit Rights *(the page competitors can't write)*

Most rental decorating advice online is written for a US audience and talks about a "security deposit" in terms that don't match UK law at all. Here's what actually applies.

**Your deposit must be protected.** In England and Wales, a landlord taking a deposit on an assured shorthold tenancy must protect it in one of three government-approved schemes:

| Scheme | Type |
|---|---|
| **Deposit Protection Service (DPS)** | Custodial and insured |
| **MyDeposits** | Custodial and insured |
| **Tenancy Deposit Scheme (TDS)** | Custodial and insured |

*Scotland and Northern Ireland operate their own separate approved schemes — check the relevant national scheme list if you rent there.*

☐ **You must be given the prescribed information** — which scheme holds it, how to get it back, and how disputes are handled. If you never received this, that's a compliance failure worth knowing about

☐ **There is a legal cap on how much can be taken.** Under the Tenant Fees Act 2019 in England, tenancy deposits are capped relative to your annual rent, and holding deposits are capped separately. Check the current cap against your own rent before assuming what you paid was permitted

☐ **Fair wear and tear is not damage.** A landlord cannot deduct for the normal deterioration that comes from living in a property. Carpet thinning in a hallway is wear. A burn in the carpet is damage. The distinction is the basis of most successful dispute outcomes

☐ **Every scheme offers free dispute resolution.** You do not need a solicitor and you do not pay to raise a dispute — the adjudicator reviews the evidence from both sides and decides

**This is why the next page matters more than anything else in this kit.**

*This is general information, current as of 2026, and not legal advice. Deposit rules differ across England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland and change over time — confirm against your scheme and the current legislation before acting on a dispute.*

## PAGE 8 — The Check-In & Check-Out Record

*Ten minutes on day one is the cheapest insurance in this entire kit.*

At move-out, every dispute comes down to one question: **was that mark already there?** Without dated proof, it's your word against your landlord's, and you are the one who loses.

**On day one, before you unpack:**

☐ Photograph every room from multiple angles — timestamps matter, so use a method that records them automatically
☐ Photograph every existing mark closely: scuffs, scratches, stains, chips, worn carpet
☐ **Cross-reference the landlord or agent's check-in inventory report** and flag anything missing or inaccurate immediately, in writing. Disputing the inventory on day one is straightforward; disputing it at move-out is not
☐ Test every plug socket with a charger
☐ Test all taps hot and cold, and check under sinks for leaks
☐ Test the oven, hob, and any included appliances
☐ Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms; note the battery dates
☐ Check every window and door opens, closes, and locks
☐ Note the condition of blinds, curtains, and any included furniture
☐ Email everything to the landlord or agent within 48 hours — email, not text
☐ Keep your own copy somewhere that isn't only your phone

**Before you hand the keys back:**

☐ Reinstall every original bulb, handle, and fitting from your labelled bags
☐ Remove all adhesive strips slowly by the release tab, at the correct angle
☐ Remove peel-and-stick wallpaper by warming it with a hairdryer on medium and peeling slowly from the top corner
☐ Photograph every room again, from the same angles as your check-in shots
☐ Attend the check-out inspection in person if you possibly can

**The paired photographs — same angle, move-in and move-out — are the single strongest piece of evidence in any deposit dispute.** That's the whole reason for page 8.
