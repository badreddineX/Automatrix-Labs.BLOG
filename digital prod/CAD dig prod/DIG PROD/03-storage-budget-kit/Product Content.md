# FILE 1 — The Storage Solutions Lookbook

## PAGE 1 — The Four Moves That Beat Everything Else

If you do nothing else in this guide, do these four. They outperform every other option on cost-to-impact, and all four are fully reversible.

| # | Move | Cost (CAD) | Why It Wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Go vertical** — shelves above eye level | ~$24 + strips | Walls above eye level are the most underused space in any apartment |
| 2 | **Claim under the bed** | ~$14–40 | Biggest single untapped area, zero floor footprint |
| 3 | **Slim velvet hangers** | ~$18 | Frees 30–40% more closet space immediately |
| 4 | **Over-door organizers** | ~$25–40 | Storage on surfaces you're not using, zero installation |

Everything else in this lookbook builds on those four.

## PAGE 5 — The No-Drill Rule

Every solution in this lookbook is reversible. That's not a stylistic choice — it's what keeps your deposit intact.

☐ **Adhesive picture-hanging strips** — check the pack's weight rating against what you're hanging; larger sizes hold several kilograms and peel off cleanly (~$10–15 CAD)
☐ **Tension rods** — closets, under sinks, window frames. Zero hardware
☐ **Freestanding over wall-mounted** — a ladder shelf or bookcase does the job of a mounted shelf with no wall damage
☐ **Over-door hardware** — hooks and organizers that hang from the door itself
☐ **Peel-and-stick** — removable backsplash, wallpaper, and hooks, all fully reversible
☐ **Stick-on LED puck lights** — closet and under-cabinet lighting with no wiring

**Before any adhesive goes up:** wipe the wall with rubbing alcohol and let it dry completely. Adhesive fails on dust and on paint that hasn't fully cured — and a failed strip takes paint with it, which is exactly the outcome you're avoiding.

## PAGE 7 — The Priority Order

Starting from zero, this sequence gives the most improvement per dollar:

1. **Under-bed storage** — biggest gain, zero floor footprint *(~$14–40)*
2. **Slim velvet hangers** — frees closet space the same day *(~$18)*
3. **Over-door organizers** — storage that takes no room at all *(~$25–40)*
4. **Vertical shelving** — the wall space above eye level *(~$24–50)*
5. **Anchor piece** — cube storage or a wardrobe for the main room *(~$79–179)*

Stop after any step and you're still ahead. This is deliberately ordered so a $50 budget and a $300 budget both end up with the highest-value items first.

## Where the Money Actually Goes

The anchor furniture — wardrobe, cube storage, ottoman, entry bench — is roughly **60–70% of the Complete tier total**. Every other line is comparatively minor. If you need to cut, cut there first (Facebook Marketplace for the large pieces) rather than shaving a few dollars off bins.

### Production notes (not part of the buyer-facing product)

- **Format:** PDF at Letter, A4, and A5, plus the budget planner as both `.xlsx` and a Google Sheets "make a copy" link. All in one ZIP
- **Build method:** same HTML/CSS → browser print → PDF pipeline as the Organization Suite. Accent theme for this product is purple. The spreadsheet is built separately, not exported from the HTML
- **The spreadsheet must actually have working formulas.** An "editable planner" that's a static table is the fastest route to a refund request — the totals and the planned-vs-actual variance need to calculate
- **Deliberate separation from the Organization Suite:** this kit is buying-intent (what to buy, what it costs, will it fit), the Suite is decluttering-intent (what to keep, where it lives, how to maintain it). Keep the copy on both listings explicit about this so they cross-sell instead of cannibalizing
- **Differentiator:** Canadian retailer specificity end to end — IKEA Canada, Canadian Tire, Dollarama, HomeSense, Wayfair Canada, Facebook Marketplace, with CAD pricing throughout. A US-authored competitor cannot produce this table
- **Verify before publishing:** every CAD price against the live retailer listing, and re-check the tier totals by summing the actual line items rather than eyeballing a round number
- **Weight-rating caution:** adhesive strip capacities are written as "check the pack rating" rather than a hard number on purpose — manufacturers change ratings per size and a wrong figure in a paid product is a real liability. Do not tighten this into a specific kilogram claim without verifying the current packaging
- **Free lead-magnet extraction:** Page 1 (the four moves that beat everything else) or the Measurement & Planning Sheet on its own — the measurement sheet is genuinely useful standalone and naturally leads into the paid buying guide
