# OFFER_AUDIT.md — The Closet & Wardrobe Reset

Audited 2026-08-04 using the Hormozi value-equation framework, grounded in the actual
product content (read in full: 12 chapters, ~6,400 words after the 2026-08-04 expansion
and typo-cleanup pass, real CAD pricing throughout).

## 1. Offer Summary

- **Who it's for:** Canadian small-space/apartment renters with limited closet space
  (SmallSpaceHome.ca readers)
- **What it promises:** a no-buy, one-afternoon system to organize clothes, shoes, and
  accessories so everything fits and the reader loves what they wear
- **How it works:** 12-chapter PDF, one closet system per chapter (full empty, sorting,
  file-fold, hangers, color order, shoes, accessories, seasonal swap, laundry, drawers,
  maintenance, mindful buying), each with a real named example, real CAD prices, and a
  checklist
- **Price:** $9 CAD

> This offer helps Canadian renters fit their whole wardrobe into a small closet using a
> proven sort-and-organize system, in one afternoon, without buying new furniture.

## 2. Overall Diagnosis

**Strengths:** genuinely actionable, step-by-step methods (the file fold, the 30-day maybe
box, the one-in-one-out rule) rather than generic decluttering platitudes. The core hook —
"a smaller wardrobe is a better wardrobe" — is a clear, differentiated angle.

**Weaknesses:** before this pass, the source manuscript had a real quality problem: dozens
of typos and garbled words throughout (e.g. "Dreshirts," "colororder," "Ue a shelf,"
"catgory," a missing line break in the Chapter 5 heading, and a stray orphaned `** The`
fragment left in Chapter 2) — the kind of visible errors that damage trust in a paid $9
product regardless of how good the underlying advice is. All of these were corrected in
this pass alongside the word-count expansion. Same structural gaps as the other CAD books
remain: no risk reversal, no explicit value stack, no fast-path for skimmers.

## 3. Value Equation Analysis

### Dream Outcome
- **Score: 7/10**
- **Issues:** Clear and desirable, but "everything fits and you love what you wear" is
  qualitative with no anchor number.
- **Fixes:** Use the "30-40% of clothes rarely or never worn" statistic (already in the
  Introduction) as the anchor — reframe the promise as reclaiming that 30-40% of space.

### Perceived Likelihood
- **Score: 7/10**
- **Issues:** Every chapter now has a specific real-world example (added in this pass).
  No external social proof yet, as this is a new product.
- **Fixes:** Same fix as the other CAD books — a short "why trust this" author-credibility
  line, reused from elsewhere on the site.

### Time Delay
- **Score: 8/10**
- **Issues:** The tagline itself promises "one afternoon" — a strong, specific time claim
  that the chapter structure genuinely supports (each chapter's core task is 15-30 minutes).
- **Fixes:** Surface "most chapters take under 30 minutes" in sales copy alongside the
  "one afternoon" tagline — reinforces rather than repeats it.

### Effort & Sacrifice
- **Score: 5/10**
- **Issues:** 12 chapters run in sequence (the guide explicitly says "work through in
  order"), which is more effort than a skimmer wants for a quick fix.
- **Fixes:** Add a "Start Here — first hour" page pointing to the highest-impact opening
  sequence (Chapters 1-2: full empty + sorting), since the guide already frames these as
  foundational and everything else builds on them.

## 4. Market Fit

- **Issues:** None — small closets in Canadian rental apartments are close to universal,
  and $9 is trivially affordable for the target buyer.
- **Fixes:** None needed.

## 5. Offer Structure

- **Issues:** Clear as a single sequential PDF, but the 30-day maybe-box method and the
  cost-per-wear calculator (Chapter 12) are separable, standalone-useful tools not named
  as bonuses anywhere in the offer framing.
- **Fixes:** Name them explicitly wherever this product is sold.

## 6. Value Stack

- **Issues:** Same gap as the rest of the CAD line — reads as "one PDF" when it contains
  at least 3 separable components: the room-by-room closet system, the 30-day maybe-box
  decision method, and the cost-per-wear buying filter.
- **Fixes:** Present as a stack on the product page:
  - The Closet & Wardrobe Reset System (12 chapters) — the core guide
  - **Bonus:** The 30-Day Maybe Box Method (removes decision paralysis when sorting)
  - **Bonus:** The Cost-Per-Wear Buying Filter (stops future closet overflow)
  - **Total value: [honest estimate] — Today: $9 CAD**

## 7. Pricing

- **Issues:** $9 CAD is reasonable but unanchored.
- **Fixes:** Anchor against the cost-per-wear math already in Chapter 12 — a single
  never-worn $50 impulse buy costs more than five copies of this book.

## 8. Messaging

- **Issues:** No factual/legal accuracy problems — this book makes no legal claims,
  unlike the No-Damage Toolkit's provincial deposit-law issue.
- **Fixes:** None needed on accuracy. Do fix the visible typos flagged above if any were
  missed in this pass — a proofread pass on the final PDF is worth doing before re-listing.

## 9. Objections & Trust

- **Issues:** No guarantee anywhere.
- **Fixes:** Add a simple guarantee line ("not useful? Email us within 30 days for a full
  refund") — standard, cheap, closes the trust gap for a $9 impulse buy.

## 10. Top Priority Fixes

1. **Add a value stack** to the product page — name the maybe-box method and cost-per-wear
   filter as explicit bonuses.
2. **Add a no-risk 30-day guarantee** — closes the trust gap.
3. **Confirm the rebuilt PDF is typo-free** — the source manuscript had a real, visible
   quality issue before this pass; verify the rebuilt PDF reads cleanly before re-listing.

## 11. Quick Wins

- Surface "most chapters take under 30 minutes" in sales copy alongside the existing
  "one afternoon" tagline.
- Anchor the $9 price against the cost-per-wear math from Chapter 12.
- Use the "30-40% of clothes rarely worn" statistic from the Introduction as the headline
  dream-outcome anchor on the sales page.
