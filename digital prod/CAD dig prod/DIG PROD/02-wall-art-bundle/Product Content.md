## BONUS PDF 1 — The Gallery Wall Layout Guide

*Four layouts, each drawn to scale with spacing measurements.*

**Layout 1 — The Grid (4 prints, equal size)**
Two by two, 5 cm gap between frames. The most forgiving layout — works above a sofa, a bed, or a console. Total footprint with 8×10" frames: roughly 55 × 65 cm.

**Layout 2 — The Vertical Pair (2 prints)**
Two prints stacked, 5 cm apart. Designed for narrow walls — beside a doorway, in a hallway, or in the dead space between a window and a corner.

**Layout 3 — The Horizontal Run (3 prints)**
Three prints in a line at eye level, 8 cm apart. Best above a sofa or a bed where the wall is wider than it is tall.

**Layout 4 — The Leaning Stack (2–3 prints, zero wall contact)**
Larger frames leaned against the wall on a shelf, console, or the floor. **This is the renter's layout** — the same visual weight as a hung gallery wall with literally nothing touching the wall.

**Universal rule:** hang the centre of the arrangement at 145–150 cm from the floor. This is standard gallery eye level, and it's the single most common mistake in home gallery walls — most people hang art too high.

## BONUS PDF 2 — The Renter-Safe Hanging Guide

*How to hang all 12 prints without leaving a single hole to argue about at move-out.*

☐ **Adhesive picture strips** — the primary method. Rated pairs hold up to roughly 3.5 kg depending on the size; check the pack rating against your frame weight and peel off cleanly at move-out (~$10–15 CAD, Canadian Tire or Amazon.ca)

☐ **Lean, don't hang** — larger frames on a shelf, console, dresser, or the floor. Zero wall contact, and it's a deliberate look, not a compromise

☐ **Picture ledge on adhesive strips** — a narrow shelf lets you swap prints seasonally without touching the wall again

☐ **Washi tape borders** — for a genuinely temporary or budget approach, frame the print directly on the wall with decorative tape. No frame cost at all

☐ **Clipboard or magnetic poster hanger** — hang from a single adhesive hook, swap the print in seconds

**Before you hang anything:** wipe the wall with rubbing alcohol and let it dry fully. Adhesive strips fail on dusty or freshly painted walls, and a failed strip is how a print ends up on the floor at 3 a.m.

**Frame sourcing in Canada:** IKEA Canada is the cheapest reliable option across all five included sizes. Dollarama carries 5×7" and 8×10" frames at $4–5. HomeSense is the best mid-range pick if you want the frames themselves to be part of the look.

## Printing Guide (include in the listing description too)

| Print Method | Cost | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Home inkjet | Paper cost only | 5×7" and 8×10" — fastest, good enough on matte cardstock |
| Staples Canada | ~$1–5 per print | 11×14" and up, reliable colour |
| Walmart / London Drugs photo | ~$2–8 | Photo-paper finish, cheapest for larger sizes |
| Local print shop | $10–25 | 16×20", best quality, worth it for the one statement piece |

**Paper recommendation:** matte or lightly textured cardstock, 200 gsm or heavier. Glossy photo paper fights the muted palette these designs use and shows glare in small rooms with a single light source.

### Production notes (not part of the buyer-facing product)

- **This is the bottleneck product.** 12 designs × 5 sizes = 60 files, each needing its own native layout rather than a stretched master. Budget the majority of the build window here
- **Generate the art, don't stock-source it.** Use the `design` / `banner-design` skills (Gemini image generation) with the palette locked across all 12 so the bundle reads as one collection. Sourcing from stock sites risks licensing problems on a product being resold
- **Palette lock is the whole product.** If the 12 designs don't share a coherent palette, this is 12 files in a folder, not a bundle — and the bundle framing is what supports the $8–12 price against $2–4 single-print competitors
- **JPG is the primary format, not PDF.** JPG at 300 DPI is what print shops and home printers handle most reliably, and it stays under Etsy's 20MB per-file cap. Ship PDF as a bonus for buyers who prefer it
- **Never pure white backgrounds** — off-white/cream prints cleanly on consumer printers; pure white exposes every calibration difference and generates refund requests
- **Differentiator:** Set C (Canadian Small-Space) plus the renter-safe hanging guide. Competitors sell print files; almost none of them ship a no-drill hanging guide written for people who can't put holes in the wall. Lead the listing previews with the leaning-stack layout — it's the visual that says "this is for renters"
- **Preview images:** mock the prints into a real small-space room shot, not on a blank white background. Buyers decide from previews before they read a word
- **Free lead-magnet extraction:** one single print (pick the strongest from Set A) at 8×10" only, or the gallery wall layout guide on its own
