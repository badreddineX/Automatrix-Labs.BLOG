# FILE 1 — The Room-by-Room System

## PAGE 1 — Start Here: Declutter Before You Organize

Before you touch a single shelf or buy a single bin, go through every room and remove what does not belong. This is the step most renters skip, and it's why the bins they bought didn't fix anything.

**The three-pile rule:**

☐ **Keep** — used in the last 6 months, has a clear purpose
☐ **Donate** — good condition, no longer needed
☐ **Trash** — broken, expired, or has no value to anyone

In a small apartment there is no room for maybes. If you have not used it in six months and cannot name a specific upcoming occasion when you will, it leaves.

**Do this before you shop for anything.** Buying storage products before deciding what to keep is the most expensive mistake in small-space organization — you end up paying to store things you were going to get rid of anyway.

## PAGE 2 — The Entryway: First In, First Out

The entryway sets the tone for the whole apartment. If it's chaotic when you walk in, the rest feels chaotic too.

**The minimal entryway system:**

☐ **Hooks** — one per person plus one spare, e.g. Command large hooks (~$12 CAD, 4-pack, Canadian Tire)
☐ **Shoe rack** — slim 2-tier, essential in Canadian winters when boots come in wet (~$20–35 CAD)
☐ **A small tray or bin** — keys, transit cards, anything that leaves with you
☐ **Nothing else** — the entryway is a transition zone, not storage

**The test:** can you grab your keys, put on shoes, and be out the door in 60 seconds? If not, the entryway needs work.

---

## PAGE 3 — The Kitchen: Zone Everything

A small kitchen requires systems, not just tidiness. Everything needs a place that makes sense for how you actually cook.

**The four kitchen zones:**

| Zone | What Lives There | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Prep** | Cutting boards, knives, bowls, mixing tools | Near the main counter space |
| **Cooking** | Pots, pans, spatulas, oils | Near the stove |
| **Pantry** | Dry goods and canned goods, grouped by category | Cabinet or shelving |
| **Cleaning** | Dish soap, sponges, drying rack | Near the sink |

Once you know the zones, reorganize what you already have to match them. You'll find many things are in the wrong place — fixing that costs nothing.

**Then clear the counters completely.** Everything goes except the coffee maker (if used daily), the knife strip, and one plant. A clear counter makes a small kitchen look and feel significantly larger than any organizer will.

**High-impact kitchen products:**

☐ Stackable clear pantry bins — see everything, stop buying duplicates (~$5–8 CAD each, Amazon.ca; buy 6–8 for a full pantry)
☐ Bamboo drawer dividers, adjustable (~$22–32 CAD/set, Amazon.ca)
☐ Wall pegboard for utensils, e.g. IKEA SKADIS (~$15 CAD) — frees a full drawer
☐ Over-door cabinet organizer for spices and foil (~$15–22 CAD)
☐ Rolling 3-tier cart, e.g. IKEA RASKOG (~$39 CAD) — extra surface that tucks away

## PAGE 4 — The Bedroom: Closet First, Then Everything Else

The bedroom serves two purposes: sleep and storage. Most small bedrooms do the storage part badly, which affects the sleep part.

**Start with the closet:**

☐ **Slim velvet hangers throughout** — frees 30–40% more hanging space (~$18 CAD for 50, Amazon.ca). *If you do only one thing on this page, do this one.*
☐ **Second tension rod** below the existing one for shirts and jackets (~$15–22 CAD) — five minutes, no tools
☐ **Over-door organizer** on the closet door for shoes and accessories (~$25–35 CAD)
☐ **Shelf dividers** so folded stacks stop toppling (~$18–26 CAD for 6)

**Then claim the under-bed space** — the biggest untapped storage in most apartments:

☐ Flat lidded bins or zippable bags, e.g. IKEA SKUBB (~$14 CAD for 2)
☐ Bed risers if your frame sits too low (~$25 CAD adds ~15 cm clearance)
☐ Vacuum bags for off-season winter gear — compresses to about a quarter of the volume (~$25–30 CAD for 6)

**The nightstand:** lamp, charger, water, one book. Anything else is clutter. A floating shelf on Command strips works better than a full nightstand in a small bedroom — no floor footprint.

## PAGE 5 — Living Room + Bathroom

**Living Room — three steps, in order:**

1. **Assign a home to everything.** Every object needs a specific place it lives. Remotes in one spot. Chargers out of sight. If something doesn't have a home, either give it one or remove it.
2. **Use closed storage.** Open shelves look great styled but collect clutter fast. Cube storage with fabric inserts (e.g. IKEA KALLAX 4-cube ~$79 CAD + inserts ~$6 each) gives accessible storage that stays tidy — one cube per category.
3. **Run the 10-minute evening reset.** Faster than a weekly clean and keeps the space feeling organized all the time.

**Bathroom — go vertical, keep surfaces clear:**

☐ Over-toilet shelf — uses dead space, adds 2–3 shelves (~$40–70 CAD)
☐ Over-door organizer for hair tools and products
☐ Drawer dividers in the vanity
☐ One small tray on the counter for daily-use items only — everything else in a drawer

**Goal:** only items used every single day stay on the counter.

## PAGE 6 — The Maintenance System

Getting organized is one thing. Staying organized is the real challenge — and it's where most systems fall apart.

**Three habits that actually work:**

☐ **One in, one out** — every time something new comes in, something old leaves. New book arrives, an old one goes to donation. Keeps total quantity flat, permanently.

☐ **10-minute evening reset** — every surface cleared, dishes done, clothes hung or in laundry, nothing on the floor. Takes 10 minutes when done consistently; takes an hour when it isn't.

☐ **Weekly zone check** — one zone per week, 15 minutes. Confirm things are in their right place and nothing has accumulated that shouldn't be there.

Consistency matters more than any bin or shelf you can buy.

## PAGE 7 — The Priority Order & Budget

If you're starting from zero, this order gives the most improvement per dollar spent:

1. **Declutter** — remove what doesn't belong before organizing what does *(free)*
2. **Bedroom closet** — slim hangers + tension rod *(~$35 CAD)*
3. **Under-bed** — the highest-gain move with zero floor footprint *(~$14–40 CAD)*
4. **Entryway** — hooks and a shoe rack, sets the tone for everything *(~$32–47 CAD)*
5. **Kitchen** — zone it first, then add pantry bins *(~$30–48 CAD)*
6. **Living room** — closed storage + the 10-minute reset habit *(~$79–110 CAD)*

**One room at a time. Finish it completely before moving to the next.**

# FILE 3 — The Seasonal Reset Checklist

*Four quarterly resets. Each takes an afternoon, not a weekend.*

## Winter Reset (January)

☐ Post-holiday declutter — box decorations by category (lights, ornaments, textiles) and label the box
☐ Closet swap — move summer clothes to under-bed storage or vacuum bags
☐ Entryway deep clean — salt and slush damage accumulates fast; check the shoe rack and mats
☐ Pantry audit — check expiry dates, consolidate duplicates into labeled bins
☐ Test smoke and carbon monoxide detectors, note battery dates

## Spring Reset (April)

☐ Reverse the closet swap — winter coats and boots into storage
☐ Deep clean behind and under furniture
☐ Redo your move-in photo record — an annual "state of the unit" refresh that re-arms your evidence if a deposit dispute ever comes up
☐ Wash or replace shower curtain liner, bath mats, and window coverings
☐ Full kitchen reset — empty every cabinet, wipe, re-zone, restock into bins

## Summer Reset (July)

☐ Donation run — anything from the January and April piles that's still sitting there
☐ Balcony or outdoor space setup if you have one
☐ Check on lease renewal timing — know your notice period before it auto-renews
☐ Fan and air-conditioner clean before the first heat wave
☐ Digital declutter — photos, downloads, and the cable drawer

## Autumn Reset (October)

☐ Heating check before the first cold snap — report anything not working, in writing
☐ Seasonal swap back — winter bedding, coats, boots out of storage
☐ Weatherproofing — draft stoppers, window film, thicker curtains
☐ Restock the entryway for winter — boot tray, extra mat, umbrella spot
☐ Pre-holiday declutter so December doesn't land on a full apartment
