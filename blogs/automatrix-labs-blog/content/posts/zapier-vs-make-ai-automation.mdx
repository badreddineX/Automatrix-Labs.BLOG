If you've searched for an automation tool this year, you've probably landed on the same two names over and over: Zapier and Make. Both let you connect apps, trigger workflows, and now drop AI steps right into the middle of them. But they're built differently, priced differently, and honestly reward different kinds of users.

I've built real workflows in both, from simple lead-notification zaps to multi-branch client onboarding flows with AI summarization steps. Here's the honest breakdown of where each one wins.

**Key Takeaways**
- Zapier has a larger app library and a simpler, more beginner-friendly builder, per Zapier's own app directory.
- Make's visual, node-based canvas is more powerful for complex, branching workflows, and it's often cheaper as usage scales up.
- Both platforms now let you insert AI steps, like summarizing text or drafting replies, directly into automations using OpenAI, Anthropic, or their own AI tools.
- Your technical comfort and budget matter more than any single feature when choosing between them.

## What's the Real Difference Between Zapier and Make?

Zapier uses a linear, step-by-step "Zap" builder, while Make uses a visual, node-based canvas where you can branch, loop, and route data in multiple directions at once, according to each platform's own documentation. That structural difference shapes almost everything else about how they feel to use.

Zapier's format is closer to filling out a form: trigger, then action, then another action, in a straight line. It's intuitive almost immediately, even if you've never automated anything before. Make's canvas looks more like a flowchart, with modules you drag, connect, and arrange freely across the screen.

That flowchart layout is exactly why Make can do things Zapier struggles with. Want a workflow that splits into three different paths depending on a condition, then merges the results back together? Make handles that natively. In Zapier, you'd typically need multiple separate Zaps or its Paths feature stacked awkwardly to get similar behavior.

The tradeoff is learning curve. Zapier gets a first automation running in minutes. Make usually takes longer to click, especially if you've never worked with node-based tools like this before. Neither approach is objectively better, they're built for different comfort levels.

## Which Tool Has Better App and AI Integrations?

Zapier connects to a noticeably larger number of apps than Make, according to each platform's published integration directory, which matters most if you rely on niche or lesser-known software. Both platforms support AI steps, but they implement them a bit differently.

Zapier offers built-in AI actions, like "Create with AI," alongside native integrations for OpenAI, Anthropic's Claude, and Google's Gemini models. You can drop an AI step into a Zap to summarize an email, categorize a support ticket, or draft a reply, without writing any code. It also has "AI by Zapier," an assistant that can help build automations from a plain-language description.

Make offers similar AI module integrations for the major model providers, plus its own AI Agents feature for more autonomous, multi-step decision-making within a scenario. In practice, Make's AI steps sit more naturally inside its branching logic, since you can route AI output down different paths based on what the model returns.

Here's the honest takeaway from testing both: if your stack leans on mainstream apps like Gmail, Slack, and Google Sheets, either tool covers you fine. If you rely on smaller or industry-specific software, check Zapier's app directory first, it's the safer bet for coverage.

## How Do Zapier and Make Compare on Pricing?

Zapier and Make both offer free tiers and scale pricing by usage, but Make is generally the more budget-friendly option at higher volumes, based on each company's own published pricing pages. Zapier's task-based pricing can climb quickly once your automations run often.

Zapier counts every completed action as a "task" and prices plans around monthly task allowances, with limits on things like multi-step Zaps and certain premium apps reserved for paid tiers. Make counts usage in "operations," where each module execution in a scenario uses one operation, and its plans have historically offered more headroom for the price on paper.

Neither company publishes exact numbers that stay fixed for long, both platforms have adjusted pricing and tier structures over time, so it's worth checking current rates directly before committing. As a rough rule from testing both: light, occasional automation tends to cost about the same either way, but heavy daily usage is where Make's pricing structure usually pulls ahead.

## Who Should Actually Choose Zapier vs Make?

Freelancers who want the simplest setup, rely on mainstream apps, and automate occasionally are usually better off with Zapier, while anyone building complex, high-volume, or branching workflows should lean toward Make. Technical comfort matters as much as budget here.

If you've never built an automation before, start with Zapier. The linear builder maps closely to how most people think about a task: this happens, then that happens. You'll get something working on day one without watching a single tutorial.

If you're comfortable thinking in flowcharts, or your workflows involve multiple conditions, loops, or data transformations, Make will likely save you time once you're past the initial learning curve. It's also worth considering if you expect your automation volume to grow significantly, since operations-based pricing tends to stretch further than task-based pricing at scale.

We've found that a lot of freelancers actually end up using both, Zapier for quick, simple connections between everyday apps, and Make for the one or two heavier workflows that genuinely need the extra control. There's no rule saying you have to pick just one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is Make cheaper than Zapier?**
A: Generally, yes at higher usage volumes, since Make's operations-based pricing tends to offer more headroom per dollar than Zapier's task-based model, based on each platform's published pricing. Light users often see similar costs on either tool.

**Q: Does Zapier or Make have more integrations?**
A: Zapier has a larger published app directory, making it the stronger choice if you rely on niche or industry-specific software, according to Zapier's own integration listing. Make covers most mainstream apps as well.

**Q: Can both Zapier and Make use AI models like ChatGPT?**
A: Yes, both platforms offer native integrations with OpenAI, Anthropic, and other model providers, letting you add AI steps like summarizing or drafting text directly into a workflow, per each company's documentation.

**Q: Which tool is easier to learn for beginners?**
A: Zapier is generally easier to learn first, thanks to its linear, step-by-step builder. Make's visual canvas is more powerful but takes longer to feel intuitive if you've never used node-based tools before.

**Q: Can I use Zapier and Make together?**
A: Yes, many freelancers use Zapier for quick, simple app connections and Make for more complex, branching workflows. There's no restriction on running both platforms side by side.

## The Verdict

Neither tool is universally "better." Zapier wins on simplicity, app coverage, and getting your first automation running fast. Make wins on power, flexibility, and value once your workflows get complex or your usage grows.

The honest way to decide: think about your actual workflows, not the feature list. If you're connecting a handful of everyday apps occasionally, Zapier's simplicity will save you more time than Make's extra power. If you're building branching, multi-condition workflows that run often, Make's canvas and pricing structure are likely worth the steeper learning curve.
