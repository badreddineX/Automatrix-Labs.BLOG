The kitchen is the hardest room to organize in a small apartment. Unlike the bedroom or living room, it requires a lot of specific tools and supplies to function — cutting boards, pots, pans, dry goods, spices, cleaning products, small appliances — and most rental apartment kitchens were not designed with much storage in mind.

My Toronto apartment kitchen is galley-style: about 2 × 3 metres, one counter, 6 upper cabinets, and a pantry. I have organized it three times. Here is what the working version looks like.

## The Core Idea: Zone Your Kitchen by Task

Before buying any organization products, define the zones:

- **Prep zone** — cutting board, knives, mixing bowls, near the primary counter space
- **Cooking zone** — pots, pans, spatulas, near the stove
- **Pantry zone** — dry goods, canned goods, organized by category
- **Drinks zone** — coffee maker, kettle, mugs
- **Cleaning zone** — dish soap, sponge, drying rack, near the sink

Once you know what goes in each zone, every organization product decision becomes much clearer.

## 1. Clear the Counter Completely First

The first step of kitchen organization is clearing the counter of everything that does not belong there. Most apartment kitchen counters accumulate mail, bags, phone chargers, and random objects that should be elsewhere.

Your counter is for food preparation. The only things that belong there permanently are the items you use daily: coffee maker, toaster, a small fruit bowl if you eat fruit daily, and one plant if you have one.

Everything else lives in a drawer, cabinet, or another room.

## 2. Clear Stackable Pantry Bins

Loose pantry items — open bags of flour, pasta packages, random cans — create chaos in any cabinet. Clear stackable bins group similar items together and let you see everything at a glance.

**Categories to create:**
- Pasta and grains
- Baking supplies
- Snacks
- Canned goods
- Breakfast items

**Cost:** ~$5–$8 CAD each on Amazon.ca or IKEA KUGGIS (~$4–$6 CAD each). Six to eight bins transforms any apartment pantry.

## 3. IKEA SKADIS Pegboard for Utensils

Hang a small pegboard on the wall beside or above the stove. It holds spatulas, ladles, tongs, whisks, and measuring cups — freeing an entire drawer for other purposes.

**Cost:** IKEA SKADIS 56 × 56 cm pegboard: ~$25 CAD. Hooks and bins: ~$3–$8 each.

**Renter installation:** Command Large Picture Hanging Strips (3–4 pairs) mount the pegboard without screws for lighter loads. For heavier pegboards, use proper wall anchors with landlord approval.

## 4. Tension Rod Under the Sink

The under-sink cabinet is usually wasted space — products pile up on the floor with no organization. A horizontal tension rod strung across the cabinet lets you hang spray bottles by their triggers, freeing the entire cabinet floor for bins and stacked products.

**Cost:** ~$8–$12 CAD at Canadian Tire or Dollar stores

Add two stackable bins on the cabinet floor: one for cleaning products, one for extra sponges and soap. Total cost: ~$25 CAD for a completely organized under-sink cabinet.

## 5. Vertical Storage for Flat Items

Cutting boards, baking sheets, and pot lids stored horizontally in a stack are frustrating to access. Store them vertically instead — each one individually accessible.

**Options:**
- IKEA FLUNS dish drying rack used inside a cabinet as a vertical file: ~$10–$15 CAD
- A magazine file: ~$8–$12 CAD from IKEA
- A dedicated pan organizer: ~$20–$35 CAD on Amazon.ca

This single change makes a big difference in daily cooking — no more removing the entire stack to get to one item.

## 6. Over-Door Pantry Organizer

The inside of your pantry cabinet door or any kitchen cabinet door is unused storage. An adjustable over-door organizer holds spices, condiments, foil, plastic wrap, and other small items.

**Cost:** ~$15–$25 CAD on Amazon.ca for an adjustable 3–5 tier organizer

In a small kitchen this can free up an entire cabinet shelf for other uses.

## 7. Lazy Susan for Corner Cabinets

Corner cabinets in small kitchens are notoriously difficult to use. Items get pushed to the back and forgotten. A turntable (lazy Susan) in a corner cabinet means every item is accessible with one rotation.

**Cost:** ~$12–$25 CAD at Amazon.ca or Canadian Tire for a turntable. ~$20–$40 CAD for a two-tier version.

Also useful inside pantry cabinets for spices, oils, and condiments.

## 8. Drawer Dividers for Every Drawer

Most kitchen drawers become junk drawers within weeks because they have no internal structure. Dividers create specific compartments for specific items.

**Adjustable bamboo drawer dividers:** ~$22–$32 CAD for a set on Amazon.ca. Fit most standard kitchen drawer sizes and are adjustable as needs change.

**What to put where:**
- Utensil drawer: spatulas, ladles, whisks, tongs
- Cutlery drawer: organized compartment insert (~$8–$15 CAD)
- "Everything else" drawer: batteries, twist ties, etc. — this is the only junk drawer allowed

## 9. KORKEN Jars for Dry Goods

IKEA KORKEN glass jars with lids store dry goods in a way that is both functional and attractive. Pasta, rice, oats, sugar, flour, lentils — in clear glass jars you can see exactly what you have.

This also prevents the common apartment pantry problem: discovering three open packages of pasta in different states.

**Cost:** IKEA KORKEN jars from ~$3–$8 CAD each depending on size. A set of 6–8 jars costs ~$30–$50 CAD.

## 10. Maximize Vertical Cabinet Space With Shelf Risers

Inside standard kitchen cabinets there is often unused vertical space between shelves. A shelf riser (a simple step-up insert) creates a second level within the same cabinet.

**Cost:** ~$15–$25 CAD at Amazon.ca or Canadian Tire for a set

Use for plates and bowls (plates on the riser, cups on the shelf below), or for stacking food items with better access.

## 11. Magnetic Spice Rack on the Fridge

If counter or cabinet space is tight, a magnetic spice rack on the side of the fridge uses space that would otherwise be completely unused.

**Cost:** magnetic spice containers and rack: ~$25–$50 CAD on Amazon.ca

Labels on the lids let you see everything when looking down from above.

## 12. Small Appliance Audit

Countertop appliances take up a disproportionate amount of space in a small apartment kitchen. Do an honest audit:

- Used weekly: keep on the counter
- Used monthly: store in a lower cabinet
- Used rarely or not at all: donate or sell

Most apartment dwellers have an air fryer, toaster, coffee maker, kettle, and one or two other appliances on their counter simultaneously. In a small kitchen this leaves almost no working space.

Keep only what you use weekly on the counter. Everything else goes in storage.

## 13. Wall-Mounted Paper Towel Holder

A paper towel roll sitting on the counter takes up surprising space. A wall-mounted holder keeps it off the counter and accessible.

Command strips can mount a lightweight paper towel holder on the wall or the side of a cabinet without screws.

**Cost:** ~$10–$20 CAD for a wall-mounted holder at Canadian Tire or Amazon.ca

## 14. The Maintenance Habit: Clean as You Go

Kitchen organization falls apart when washing up is delayed. In a small apartment kitchen, even one day of dishes in the sink makes the kitchen feel chaotic and unusable.

**The rule:** clean as you go during cooking, and wash all dishes immediately after eating. In a small kitchen this takes 10–15 minutes and keeps the space always functional and organized.

If you have a dishwasher: run it when it's full, empty it immediately when done. A dishwasher with clean dishes inside is a clean dishwasher that's not being used.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I organize a small kitchen with no storage?**
Zone your kitchen by task. Use every vertical surface: pegboard for utensils, over-door organizer for spices, tension rod under the sink for spray bottles. Store flat items vertically. Use the top of the fridge for rarely-used items. Audit appliances and keep only frequently-used ones on the counter. It is about using the space that exists, not wishing for more.

**What are the best small kitchen organization products in Canada?**
Clear stackable pantry bins (~$5–$8 each on Amazon.ca), IKEA SKADIS pegboard (~$25 CAD), tension rod for under-sink (~$8–$12 CAD), bamboo drawer dividers (~$25 CAD), and IKEA KORKEN jars (~$3–$8 each). Total under $130 CAD for a completely organized kitchen.

**How do I organize a pantry cabinet in a small apartment?**
Group everything by category into clear stackable bins. Use lazy Susans for corner cabinets. Add an over-door organizer for the pantry cabinet door. Label everything so it goes back in the right place. First step: remove everything from the pantry, throw out expired items, then put back only what belongs.

**How do I keep a small kitchen organized long-term?**
Two habits: clean as you go while cooking, and put everything back in its designated spot immediately after use. The organization works when everything has a specific home. When things get left on the counter or in the wrong drawer, the system breaks down quickly in a small space.
