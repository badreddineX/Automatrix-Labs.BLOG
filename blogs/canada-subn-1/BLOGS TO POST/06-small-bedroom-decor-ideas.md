## TL;DR
- 13 small bedroom decor ideas for Canadian apartment renters
- Focus on light, vertical space, and smart furniture choices
- All renter-friendly — no permanent changes required
- Works in bedrooms from 80 to 140 sq ft

---

The bedroom is the room most people give up on in a small apartment. "It's just for sleeping," the thinking goes, so why bother?

But your bedroom is where you start and end every day. A well-decorated small bedroom feels like a retreat, not a closet. And in a Canadian winter when you are spending a lot of time inside, it matters more than you think.

I have redesigned my bedroom in my 510 sq ft Toronto apartment twice. Here is what I have learned.

## 1. Choose the Right Bed Frame — It Matters More Than You Think

The bed takes up most of the floor space in a small bedroom. The frame you choose changes how the whole room feels.

**What works in a small bedroom:**
- **Beds with storage drawers** — IKEA NORDLI with drawers, ~$399–$699 CAD. You get an entire dresser worth of storage with no additional floor footprint
- **Low platform beds** — lower bed height makes the ceiling feel higher and the room larger
- **Beds with visible legs** — you can see the floor underneath, which visually expands the space

**What to avoid:** oversized headboards, dark upholstery, and beds with no storage when you are short on space.

## 2. Put the Bed in the Right Position

The layout of a small bedroom determines everything else. Most small bedrooms have one obvious configuration: bed against the longest wall, centred.

This works for a reason — it leaves maximum floor space on either side and at the foot. If you have a window on that wall, position the bed beneath it or to one side.

What doesn't work: pushing the bed into a corner where you climb in from one side. You save floor space but lose functionality and the room feels awkward.

## 3. Hang Curtains From Ceiling to Floor

Even in a small bedroom with short windows, curtains hung from as close to the ceiling as possible — falling all the way to the floor — make the room feel significantly taller.

This is one of the most impactful small bedroom decor changes you can make for under $50 CAD.

- Curtain rod: IKEA RÄCKA or KVARTAL, mounted 5–10 cm below the ceiling
- Curtains: IKEA LILL sheers (~$10 CAD per pair) or heavier MERETE panels (~$40 CAD per pair)
- For blackout: IKEA MAJGULL or similar blackout panels (~$30–$50 CAD)

## 4. Use Light, Neutral Colours

Dark walls in a small bedroom make it feel like a cave. Light walls — white, cream, warm grey, or pale sage — reflect light and make the space feel open.

Since most rental apartments have white or off-white walls already, work with what you have:
- Add warmth through bedding (cream, terracotta, dusty pink)
- Add texture through a chunky knit throw or linen duvet cover
- Avoid very dark or very busy duvet covers — they visually shrink the bed and the room

If you want colour without painting, a feature wall of removable peel-and-stick wallpaper behind the headboard changes the whole room with zero landlord permission required. Brands available on Amazon.ca include RoomMates and NuWallpaper, ~$40–$80 CAD per roll.

## 5. Replace the Nightstand With a Floating Shelf

A conventional nightstand takes up floor space and often makes a small bedroom feel crowded. A floating shelf mounted to the wall at nightstand height gives you the same surface with zero floor footprint.

- IKEA LACK shelf + Command strips: ~$15 CAD for the shelf, $12–$15 CAD for Command strips
- Holds: lamp, phone, water glass, book — everything you actually need at bedside
- Pair it with a small wall-mounted clip light (~$25–$40 CAD) so the lamp takes up zero shelf space

## 6. Add a Full-Length Mirror

A full-length mirror on the wall or leaning against it does two things: it reflects light back into the room and creates visual depth.

In a small bedroom this is one of the fastest, cheapest ways to make the space feel bigger. IKEA HOVET floor mirror (~$279 CAD) is the classic choice. For a budget option, lean a narrower mirror against the wall — IKEA has options from ~$60 CAD.

Place it:
- On the wall opposite the window to reflect natural light
- On the back of the bedroom door (over-door mounted)
- Leaning against the wall beside the closet

## 7. Maximize the Closet

A small bedroom closet that isn't organized properly is wasted space. A properly organized closet can hold more than many dressers.

**Renter-friendly closet upgrades:**
- Slim velvet hangers throughout — frees 30–40% more hanging space. ~$18 CAD for 50-pack on Amazon.ca
- Second tension rod below the existing rod — doubles hanging capacity for shirts and jackets
- Over-door organizer on the inside of the closet door — shoes, accessories, bags
- IKEA SKUBB shelf dividers — keep folded stacks from toppling

If the closet has enough depth, an IKEA SKUBB box system on the shelf keeps smaller items organized without adding any permanent structure.

## 8. Choose a Rug That Grounds the Bed

A rug beneath the bed anchors it and makes the room feel intentional rather than like furniture floating on bare floor.

**Sizing options:**
- **One rug centred under the bed, extending 50–60 cm on all three sides** — the ideal, works best with queen and double beds
- **Two runners on either side of the bed** — good for a room that's too narrow for a large rug
- **One runner at the foot of the bed** — minimal option, still better than bare floor

Light neutral rugs in natural fibre (jute, cotton flat-weave) keep the room feeling open. Darker or patterned rugs work but can visually shrink the space.

IKEA flat-weave rugs from ~$59 CAD. Wayfair Canada for more options.

## 9. Edit Ruthlessly — The Bedroom Needs Less

Every extra object in a small bedroom increases visual noise and makes the space feel cramped. The bedroom has one purpose: rest. It does not need to double as a workspace, a storage room, or a display area.

Audit what's in your bedroom:
- Only furniture that serves sleep or dressing
- Nightstand with maximum 3 items
- Dresser top with maximum 3–4 items
- No chair that's just a clothes holder
- No desk if possible (work in the living zone instead)

The hardest edit: the chair in the corner where clothes pile up. If you catch yourself using it as a clothes rack, remove it.

## 10. Layer the Bedding

This is a decor tip, not a storage tip, and it matters. Well-layered bedding makes a bed look intentional and the room feel put together — even when everything else is simple.

**The layer formula:**
- Fitted sheet
- Flat sheet or thin blanket
- Duvet or comforter — in a light, neutral cover
- Two sleeping pillows in matching cases
- Two or three decorative cushions in a contrasting colour or texture
- A throw folded at the foot or draped over one corner

This costs less than $100 CAD if you choose IKEA or HomeSense bedding — and it transforms the feel of the room every time you make the bed.

## 11. Go Vertical With Decor and Plants

Vertical decor draws the eye upward and makes the ceiling feel higher. In a small bedroom this means:

- Tall floor plants in corners — fiddle leaf fig, snake plant, or tall monstera
- Art hung at eye level or slightly above — not floating in the middle of the wall
- Floating shelves going upward in a vertical stack — display objects at multiple heights

A tall plant in the corner opposite the window takes up minimal floor space and adds a completely different energy to the room.

## 12. Scent Creates Atmosphere

Your bedroom should smell good — not heavily fragranced, but clean and calming. This is part of the room's decor even though it is not something you can photograph.

- A reed diffuser on the nightstand or dresser — consistent subtle scent without any attention. ~$20–$40 CAD at HomeSense or Amazon.ca
- Linen spray on the pillowcase before bed — lavender is the classic
- An essential oil diffuser doubles as a small humidifier in dry Canadian winters, ~$30–$50 CAD

## 13. Create a Small Focal Point

A focal point gives the bedroom a sense of design rather than just furniture placement. It does not need to be elaborate.

**Simple focal points for a small bedroom:**
- A headboard (real or DIY — a large framed piece of fabric behind the bed works)
- A removable wallpaper panel on the wall behind the headboard
- A gallery wall of 4–6 small frames above the nightstand
- A single large piece of art centred on the wall behind the bed

## The Small Bedroom Decor Priority Order

1. **Bed position and storage** — get the bed right first
2. **Curtains high** — biggest visual impact for minimal cost
3. **Full-length mirror** — adds light and depth
4. **Maximize the closet** — slim hangers, second rod, over-door organizer
5. **Layer the bedding** — makes the whole room look finished

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I decorate a small bedroom on a budget in Canada?**
Start with curtains hung from ceiling to floor (~$40–$60 CAD at IKEA), add a full-length leaning mirror (~$60–$80 CAD), upgrade bedding with a neutral duvet cover from IKEA or HomeSense (~$40–$80 CAD), and replace a bulky nightstand with a floating wall shelf (~$15–$30 CAD total with Command strips). Under $200 CAD for a complete transformation.

**What colours make a small bedroom look bigger?**
White, warm off-white, cream, pale grey, and light sage all work well. They reflect light instead of absorbing it. For accent colour use bedding and textiles — this way you can change the colour of the room without touching the paint.

**How do I add storage to a small bedroom without a dresser?**
Under-bed storage with a storage bed frame (IKEA NORDLI ~$399+ CAD) or bed risers + flat bins. Over-door organizers in the closet. Floating shelves above the nightstand. A slim hanging closet organizer for folded items. These combined replace what a dresser would hold without the floor space.

**What size rug is right for a small bedroom?**
A rug that extends 50–60 cm on each side and at the foot of the bed is ideal. For a double bed in a narrow room, two runners on either side work well. Avoid rugs smaller than the bed footprint — they look awkward and do not help the room feel anchored.
