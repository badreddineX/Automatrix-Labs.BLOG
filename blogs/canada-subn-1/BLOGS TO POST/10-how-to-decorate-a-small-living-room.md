## TL;DR

- Works for renters in any Canadian city — no permanent changes required
- Covers layout, colour, furniture, textiles, lighting, and storage together
- Budget options throughout, real CAD prices

---

Most decorating advice tells you what to buy. This guide tells you what order to do things — because the sequence matters as much as the choices.

Decorating a small living room without a plan usually ends with a room that feels crowded and incoherent, where each individual purchase seemed fine but nothing works together. I know because I did it twice in my Toronto apartment before I figured out the actual sequence.

Here is the process that works.

## Step 1: Clear the Room and Measure Everything

Before you make any decisions or buy anything, start with a blank slate.

Remove or push everything to the edges. Clean the floors. Now look at the actual space you have. Most people have never seen their living room empty — and the dimensions are usually different from what they assumed.

**Measure:**
- Room length and width
- Window placement and size
- Door swing clearance
- Distance from window to adjacent wall

Then sketch a simple floor plan on paper. This does not need to be precise — just proportional. You will use this to test furniture arrangements before moving anything heavy.

## Step 2: Decide on a Layout

In most small living rooms, there are only two or three viable furniture arrangements. Find the one that leaves the most floor space clear and maintains a path through the room.

**The most common small living room layout:**
- Sofa against the longest wall or perpendicular to the window
- Coffee table 45–60 cm in front of the sofa
- TV or focal point on the wall opposite the sofa
- No furniture blocking the path between the entry and the far end of the room

Test your layout on paper first. Once you know where the sofa goes, you know where the rug goes, where the side table goes, and where the lamp goes.

## Step 3: Choose a Colour Palette

Before buying furniture or decor, decide on a three-colour palette. This prevents purchasing decisions that look fine individually but clash when combined.

**A palette has three roles:**
- **Neutral base** — the walls (usually white in a rental), large furniture, rug
- **Main colour** — the colour that appears most in textiles (cushions, throws, curtains)
- **Accent colour** — used in small doses: plants, art, a cushion or two

Example palettes for a small Canadian living room:
- Warm minimalist: cream walls + warm terracotta main + forest green accent
- Nordic calm: white walls + dusty blue main + natural wood accent
- Modern neutral: white walls + charcoal main + rust accent

Stick to the palette when buying anything. It makes decisions faster and the room more cohesive.

## Step 4: Get the Sofa Right

The sofa is the most important furniture decision in the living room. It determines the scale of everything else.

**Rules for a small living room sofa:**
- Maximum 185–200 cm wide for a small room
- Legs visible — no platform base
- Light or neutral upholstery — beige, cream, light grey
- Fabric that matches your lifestyle — easy-clean if you have pets

**Canadian options:**
- IKEA SÖDERHAMN 2-seat: ~$899 CAD, modular, removable covers
- Structube NORA: from ~$699 CAD, clean lines, compact
- HomeSense: check for discounted options, usually $600–$1,200 CAD

If the sofa you want is too large for the room — do not buy it. No amount of decorating fixes an oversized sofa.

## Step 5: Add the Rug

The rug anchors the living room and makes it feel like a proper zone rather than furniture floating on a bare floor.

**Sizing rule:** front legs of the sofa and chairs must be on the rug. A 160 × 230 cm rug is the minimum for most small living rooms.

**What to look for:**
- Light neutral colour (cream, natural, pale grey) to keep the floor feeling open
- Low pile — easier to clean, works better under furniture
- Natural fibre (jute, cotton) or low-pile synthetics from IKEA

**Cost:** IKEA flat-weave rugs from ~$59 CAD. Wayfair Canada for a wider selection.

![Neutral jute rug anchoring a small living room with front legs of sofa on the rug](/images/blog-10-img2.jpg)

## Step 6: Hang Curtains High

Curtains hung just above the window frame make the ceiling feel lower. Curtains hung close to the ceiling — falling all the way to the floor — make it feel higher. In a small living room, this is a free (or very cheap) visual upgrade that immediately changes how the space feels.

**Where to mount:** 5–10 cm below the ceiling, or as high as the wall allows
**Length:** to the floor, with 1–2 cm clearance

IKEA curtain rods start at ~$15 CAD. Sheer LILL curtains: ~$10 CAD per pair. Heavier MERETE panels: ~$40–$60 CAD per pair.

Choose light, sheer fabric for maximum natural light. Add blackout lining only if light control is important.

## Step 7: Hang Art and Mirrors

With furniture in place, the walls look bare. This is the right time to address them — not before you know where furniture goes.

**In a small living room, two wall elements work best:**
- **One large mirror** — creates visual depth and reflects light. Place it opposite or beside the window. Lean or hang with Command strips
- **One gallery wall or large art piece** — above the sofa is the most common position

Do not fill every wall. One or two strong wall elements is enough. Filling every wall makes a small room feel busier, not better decorated.

Command Picture Strips hold up to 7.5 kg with no wall damage. IKEA art prints from the PJÄTTERYD series range from ~$20–$80 CAD.

## Step 8: Add Textiles

Textiles are the fastest way to add warmth and colour without furniture or decor. In a rental apartment where you cannot change the walls or floors, textiles do most of the decorating work.

**The small living room textile checklist:**
- 4–6 cushions on the sofa in your palette's main and accent colours
- 1–2 throw blankets draped over the sofa arm or a chair
- The rug (already covered in step 5)

Cushions are cheap to change when you want to update the look — HomeSense has cushion covers from ~$12 CAD, IKEA from ~$8 CAD. Throw blankets from IKEA POLARVIDE, ~$20 CAD.

## Step 9: Layer the Lighting

A single ceiling light makes a living room feel flat and institutional. Layer lighting to create warmth and depth.

**The three-layer approach:**
- **Ambient** — a floor lamp in one corner, pointed up. Warms the whole room
- **Task** — a table lamp on a side table for reading
- **Accent** — LED strip lights behind the TV or under shelves

All of these are plug-in, no installation. They work in rental apartments and they move with you when you leave.

Swap all bulbs to 2700K warm white if you have not already. ~$15–$25 CAD for a multipack at Canadian Tire. Single biggest lighting change you can make.

## Step 10: Add Plants

Plants are the most cost-effective decorating element in any small space. They add colour, life, and texture — and they do not add visual weight in the way furniture does.

**Best plants for small Canadian living rooms:**
- **Pothos** — thrives in low light, grows fast, nearly impossible to kill. ~$5–$15 CAD at most garden centres
- **Snake plant** — extremely low maintenance, tolerates poor light, air purifying
- **Peace lily** — one of the few flowering plants that does well indoors with limited light
- **Fiddle leaf fig** — dramatic and beautiful in a well-lit corner

One larger floor plant in a corner is more impactful than many small plants on surfaces. A corner with a tall plant and a floor lamp becomes a styled focal point.

## Step 11: Edit and Subtract

This is the final and most important step. Once the room is decorated, remove anything that does not have a clear reason to be there.

Walk to the doorway and look at the room as if you have never seen it before. What draws your eye? What bothers you? What is competing for attention?

Ruthless editing — removing a piece of furniture that crowds the room, taking a few cushions off the sofa, clearing a surface — almost always improves a small living room.

More stuff is rarely the answer.

## The Decorating Order Summary

1. Clear and measure the room
2. Decide on a layout — on paper
3. Choose a 3-colour palette
4. Get the sofa right (size, colour, legs)
5. Add the rug (right size)
6. Hang curtains from ceiling to floor
7. Hang one mirror and one wall element
8. Add textiles (cushions, throw)
9. Layer the lighting
10. Add plants
11. Edit and remove

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I start decorating a small living room with no idea where to begin?**
Measure the room first, then sketch a floor plan. Before buying anything, decide on a 3-colour palette and test your furniture layout on paper. The most common mistake is buying things without a plan and ending up with pieces that do not work together.

**What makes a small living room feel bigger?**
Light colours, a rug the right size (front legs of furniture on the rug), curtains hung from ceiling to floor, a large mirror opposite the window, and furniture with visible legs. These five changes together create a much more spacious feel.

**How much does it cost to decorate a small living room in Canada?**
Depending on what you already have: a sofa ($699–$999 CAD), rug (~$59–$150 CAD), curtains and rods (~$40–$80 CAD), art/mirror (~$60–$200 CAD), textiles (~$60–$120 CAD), and plants (~$20–$60 CAD). A complete decoration starting from empty is typically $1,000–$1,800 CAD. Incremental improvements to an existing room can be done for $200–$500 CAD.

**What furniture should I put in a small living room?**
Sofa (apartment-sized, under 200 cm), one coffee table or storage ottoman, one floor lamp, and one small side table or shelf. That is the core set. Adding more furniture risks crowding the room. Let each piece earn its place before adding the next.
