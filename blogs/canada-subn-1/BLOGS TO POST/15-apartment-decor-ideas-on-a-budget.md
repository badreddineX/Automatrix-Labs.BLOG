Decorating an apartment does not require a large budget. Most of the changes that make the biggest visual difference — lighting, textiles, mirrors, plants, and curtains — cost well under $100 CAD each.

The expensive mistakes are usually furniture purchases made before thinking the room through. The cheap wins are almost always about light, colour, and surface clarity.

Here is where to spend and where to skip when you're decorating a Canadian apartment on a tight budget.

## Free: Rearrange Your Furniture

Before spending anything, try a different furniture layout. Move the sofa. Turn the bed to face a different wall. Move the bookcase.

Layout changes cost nothing and often have more impact than any purchase. Most people never rearrange once the furniture is moved in — and the first arrangement is rarely the best one.

**What to test:**
- Is the sofa facing the window? Try turning it
- Is the bed pushed into a corner? Try centering it on the longest wall
- Is there furniture blocking the natural path through the room? Move it

Spend an afternoon trying different layouts before spending any money on decor.

## Free: Declutter

A decluttered room looks larger, cleaner, and more intentional. Clutter is the enemy of good decor at any budget.

Before spending anything on decor:
1. Remove everything from every surface
2. Put back only what belongs there
3. Donate, trash, or store everything else

A decluttered room with basic furniture looks better than a beautifully decorated room full of stuff. This is the single most important step and it costs nothing.

## $10–$15 CAD: Swap Your Light Bulbs

The fastest room transformation available for under $15 CAD. Replace every cool white or daylight bulb in your apartment with warm white (2700K) bulbs.

Cool white light (5000K) makes rooms feel clinical and institutional. Warm white (2700K) makes them feel cosy and inviting. The furniture, the walls, the textiles — everything looks better in warm light.

**Cost:** ~$12–$18 CAD for a multipack at Canadian Tire or Walmart Canada

Do this in every room, starting with the living room and bedroom.

## $10 CAD: A Pair of IKEA Sheer Curtains

IKEA LILL sheer curtain panels are $10 CAD per pair. Hung from ceiling to floor (using a rod from ~$15 CAD), they:
- Add softness and texture to bare windows
- Make the ceiling feel higher
- Filter natural light beautifully

In a rental apartment where you cannot change the walls, curtains are one of the highest-impact, lowest-cost changes you can make.

## $15–$20 CAD: A Couple of Plants

A pothos or snake plant from a garden centre costs $5–$15 CAD and lasts for years with minimal attention. Plants add:
- Colour (green against neutral walls)
- Life and texture
- Improved air quality in winter when windows stay closed

Start with one or two in the most-used room. A tall snake plant in a corner takes up no floor space and looks significant immediately.

## $20 CAD: Candles or a Reed Diffuser

The way an apartment smells is part of how it feels. A nice-smelling apartment feels more like a home than one that smells like takeout and cleaning products.

A simple soy candle from HomeSense ($15–$25 CAD) or a reed diffuser ($20–$35 CAD) adds atmosphere for a very low cost. Choose one scent per room and keep it consistent.

## $25–$45 CAD: An Area Rug

IKEA has flat-weave rugs from ~$59 CAD (LOHALS or TIPHEDE series). For a very small room or as an entry mat, options start around $25–$35 CAD.

A rug anchors the furniture and makes the room feel intentional rather than like furniture placed randomly. Without a rug, even well-chosen furniture looks unfinished.

If budget is tight, start with a 120 × 180 cm rug — smaller than ideal for a living room but better than nothing. Upgrade to a full-size rug when budget allows.

## $30–$50 CAD: Throw Cushions and a Blanket

The fastest way to change the colour of your living room or bedroom without any installation is new cushions and a throw.

- 2–3 cushions in your colour palette (HomeSense often has good options for $12–$20 CAD each)
- 1 throw blanket draped over the sofa arm or folded at the foot of the bed (IKEA POLARVIDE, ~$20 CAD)

These can be swapped seasonally to completely change the feel of a room for under $60 CAD.

## $35–$60 CAD: A Floor Lamp

Most rental apartments have inadequate overhead lighting. A floor lamp in a corner changes the entire atmosphere of a room — it warms the space, fills dead corner space, and eliminates the institutional feel of harsh overhead light.

IKEA floor lamps start at ~$49 CAD (HEKTAR or NYMANE series). Pair it with a warm 2700K bulb.

Add a floor lamp to the living room first, then the bedroom. These are the two rooms where lighting matters most.

## $40–$60 CAD: A Gallery Wall With Command Strips

A gallery wall does not require expensive art. It requires composition and frames.

**How to do it for under $60 CAD:**
- Print your own photos at Costco Canada (large prints for $1–$4 CAD each)
- Add a couple of IKEA prints from the PJÄTTERYD series (~$20–$40 CAD each)
- Find second-hand frames at Value Village for $2–$6 CAD each
- Hang with Command Picture Strips (no drilling required)

The gallery wall above the sofa or along the main hallway is the most visible wall space in most apartments. Making it intentional changes how the whole space feels.

## $45–$70 CAD: A Leaning Mirror

A round or rectangular mirror leaning against a wall in the living room or bedroom reflects light and creates visual depth — making the room feel bigger without any installation.

IKEA has options from ~$60 CAD. Even a basic full-length mirror leaning against the bedroom wall changes the room significantly.

## $50–$80 CAD: New Bedding

If your bedroom looks dated or uninspiring, the fastest single change is a new duvet cover and pillowcases.

IKEA has duvet covers from ~$30–$60 CAD. HomeSense often has quality duvet covers from $40–$80 CAD. A neutral cover (white, cream, soft grey) with a decorative cushion and a throw is the classic look.

The bedroom is the first thing you see in the morning and the last thing at night. Making it comfortable and attractive is worth $50–$80 CAD.

## $60–$100 CAD: A Full-Length Mirror

This is one of the highest-return investments in a small apartment. A large mirror reflects light and makes any room feel significantly bigger.

IKEA MONGSTAD full-length mirror: ~$169 CAD. HOVET: ~$279 CAD. For budget options, Amazon.ca has full-length mirrors from ~$60–$90 CAD.

Lean it against the wall in the bedroom or living room. No installation required.

## $15–$30 CAD: Update the Bathroom

The bathroom is the easiest room to improve on a very small budget.

- A new bath mat and matching hand towels: ~$20–$35 CAD total at HomeSense or IKEA
- A small plant (pothos thrives in bathroom humidity): ~$8–$12 CAD
- A coordinated soap dispenser: ~$8–$15 CAD
- A small framed print hung with Command strips: ~$15–$25 CAD

Under $80 CAD for a bathroom that looks intentional and put-together.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I decorate my apartment on a $100 CAD budget?**
Swap all bulbs to warm white (~$15 CAD), hang IKEA LILL sheers from ceiling to floor (~$25 CAD including the rod), add two plants (~$15–$20 CAD), and declutter completely for free. This $55–$60 CAD in changes makes a bigger visual difference than most expensive purchases.

**Where can I find cheap apartment decor in Canada?**
HomeSense for cushions, art, and textiles at 30–60% below retail prices. IKEA for curtains, plants, and small furniture. Value Village and Goodwill for frames and occasional furniture pieces. Amazon.ca for specific accessories. Dollar stores for organizing supplies and small accessories.

**What is the most cost-effective apartment decor upgrade?**
Changing to warm-white (2700K) bulbs throughout the apartment. Costs ~$15–$18 CAD and changes the mood of every room. This single change makes more apartments look better than almost any furniture or decor purchase.

**How do I make my apartment look expensive on a tight budget?**
Keep surfaces clear. Use a cohesive colour palette. Add one large plant and one large mirror. Hang curtains from ceiling to floor. Layer lighting with a floor lamp instead of overhead only. These five elements together create a put-together look regardless of the cost of the individual items.
