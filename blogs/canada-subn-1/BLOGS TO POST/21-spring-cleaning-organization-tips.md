## TL;DR
- Complete spring cleaning and organization guide for Canadian apartment renters
- Room-by-room checklist: living room, bedroom, kitchen, bathroom, closet
- Covers decluttering, deep cleaning, and organizing as a system
- Realistic timeline: one room per weekend, or a full weekend if you have one

---

Spring cleaning has a reputation as something you either do completely or not at all — an overwhelming annual reset that most people start enthusiastically and abandon by Saturday afternoon.

It doesn't have to work that way. When you break it into a room-by-room system with clear tasks in a logical order, spring cleaning becomes manageable, even satisfying. By the end you have an apartment that feels genuinely lighter — both physically and mentally.

I do this every April in my Toronto apartment. It takes one full weekend, and I look forward to it now.

## Before You Start: The Three-Box Method

For every room, bring three containers:
- **Keep** — used regularly, has a clear home in this room
- **Donate** — good condition, no longer needed
- **Trash** — broken, expired, cannot be donated

Go through the room with these three boxes before cleaning anything. Decluttering before cleaning prevents you from deep-cleaning things you are going to get rid of anyway.

## The Living Room

### Declutter First

- Remove everything from every flat surface: coffee table, shelves, side tables
- Take out every book, DVD, decorative object, and candle
- From the three-box test: keep, donate, or trash each item
- Pull out furniture from the wall and look at what is behind it

Common things to remove in a Canadian spring clean:
- Winter items that migrated in: gloves, scarves left on surfaces, boots near the door
- Things that "landed" over winter and stayed: chargers, random mail, boxes
- Expired candles or empty candle jars
- Books you finished and will not reread

### Deep Clean

- Vacuum all furniture including under cushions
- Wipe down all shelves and surfaces with a damp cloth before returning anything
- Clean the TV screen with a microfibre cloth (dry)
- Wipe baseboards
- Clean windows and windowsill — especially important in spring after a winter of condensation buildup
- Vacuum or mop the floor, moving furniture

### Organize and Refresh

- Return only what belongs in the living room, with intention
- Swap winter textiles for spring/summer: lighter cushion covers, a lighter throw
- Add a fresh plant or refresh an existing one
- Rearrange if the current layout is not working

## The Bedroom

### Declutter First

- Strip the bed completely
- Empty the nightstand and examine what is there
- Open the wardrobe and closet — start the seasonal clothing swap (see closet section below)
- Pull everything out from under the bed
- Clear the dresser surface

### Deep Clean

- Wash all bedding: duvet cover, pillow cases, sheets, mattress protector
- Flip or rotate the mattress if your mattress type allows it
- Vacuum the mattress surface with the upholstery attachment
- Wipe all furniture surfaces
- Clean the mirror and any glass surfaces
- Vacuum or mop under the bed and in all corners
- Wipe the inside of the nightstand drawers

### Organize and Refresh

- Return only what belongs on the nightstand: lamp, phone, water glass, book
- Remove the winter weighted blanket or duvet and store it
- Put on lighter spring bedding
- Add a spring-toned cushion or plant refresh

## The Closet: The Seasonal Clothing Swap

The closet spring clean is its own event. In a Canadian climate with real seasons, most people have significantly more clothing than they need accessible at any one time.

**The spring swap process:**

**Step 1 — Remove winter items**
- Heavy coats, wool sweaters, turtlenecks, thermal underlayers, snow boots
- Place in IKEA SKUBB under-bed bags or a labelled bin in the upper closet shelf

**Step 2 — Declutter before swapping**
Before putting spring/summer clothes back, apply the three-box test to everything:
- Does this still fit well?
- Have I worn this in the last year?
- Is it in good condition?
- Do I genuinely like wearing it?

In Canada, most people have more clothes than they think because winter layers make the closet feel full all season. Spring is when reality becomes clear.

**Step 3 — Clean the closet**
- Wipe down all shelves
- Vacuum or sweep the closet floor
- Check that all hangers are slim velvet (if not, swap: ~$18 CAD for 50-pack on Amazon.ca)

**Step 4 — Bring spring/summer clothes in**
- Organize by category: shirts, pants, dresses, jackets
- Use the front of the closet for what you wear most often
- Less-worn items to the back or the upper shelf

## The Kitchen

### Declutter First

- Remove everything from the pantry cabinet
- Check all expiry dates — throw out anything expired
- Remove everything from the counter and examine what actually needs to be there
- Go through the "junk drawer" and apply the three-box test

Common kitchen spring clean discards:
- Expired spices and condiments
- Duplicate kitchen tools (most kitchens have 3–4 wooden spoons, 2 colanders)
- Small appliances used once and forgotten
- Mismatched Tupperware with no lids
- Bags you "might use" stacked in a cabinet

### Deep Clean

- Empty and wipe all cabinet shelves — inside and out
- Clean the inside of the oven (use the self-clean cycle or a non-toxic cleaner)
- Wipe down the fridge interior: remove all items, wipe shelves and drawers, check expiry dates on everything inside
- Clean the range hood filter (often runs monthly in a small kitchen)
- Run the dishwasher on a cleaning cycle with a dishwasher cleaner tablet
- Wipe all appliance exteriors
- Clean inside the microwave

![Spring-cleaned and organized small apartment kitchen with cleared counters and cleaned appliances](/images/blog-21-img2.jpg)

### Organize and Refresh

- Return pantry items in clear bins organized by category
- Return counter appliances only if used weekly
- Organize the utensil drawer with bamboo dividers if you haven't already
- Add fresh herb plants in the kitchen windowsill: basil, mint, or chives (~$3–$6 CAD at most Canadian garden centres from April onward)

## The Bathroom

### Declutter First

- Remove everything from the vanity, under the sink, and medicine cabinet
- Check expiry dates on medications (expired medications can be returned to most Canadian pharmacies)
- Discard expired cosmetics, old products you are not using, and sample bottles you have accumulated
- Throw out old toothbrushes

### Deep Clean

- Scrub the toilet inside and out, including the base and around the floor
- Clean the shower or tub including grout lines and the shower head
- Wipe the vanity mirror and all surfaces
- Clean inside the medicine cabinet
- Wipe the floor including behind the toilet
- Wash the bath mat and any towels that need refreshing

### Organize and Refresh

- Return only what is used regularly to the vanity counter: maximum 3–5 items
- Everything else in drawers or under-sink storage
- Refresh the towels and bath mat if they are worn (~$20–$30 CAD at HomeSense or IKEA for new set)
- Add a small plant if bathroom light allows

## The Entry

### Declutter

- Remove all winter footwear that will no longer be worn
- Remove winter coats and store them
- Clear any accumulated items that ended up near the door

### Deep Clean

- Wipe down the shoe rack or bench
- Wash the entry mat or replace it (~$20–$35 CAD for a new spring mat)
- Clean the door surface and hardware

### Refresh

- Spring/summer shoes go on the rack, winter boots go in a labelled bin in the closet
- Add a small plant or fresh flowers in the entry if there is space

## The Donation Run and the Trash

After every room is done, you will have two boxes: donate and trash.

**Donation in Canada:**
- Salvation Army — pickup available in many cities
- Goodwill — drop-off at any location
- Value Village — clothing and household items
- Local Buy Nothing groups on Facebook — good for furniture and items that sell slowly at thrift stores
- Furniture Bank (Toronto and other cities) — for large furniture items for families transitioning from shelter

**Trash:**
- Regular recyclables in the blue bin
- Electronics at a municipal e-waste depot (most Canadian cities have these)
- Medications at the pharmacy (Canadian pharmacies take back unused medications at no charge)
- Expired aerosols at a household hazardous waste facility

Do the donation run on the same weekend as the clean. If donations sit in bags in the hallway for a week, there is a significant chance of unpacking them.

## The Post-Clean Maintenance Plan

Spring cleaning is wasted if the apartment returns to its previous state within three months.

**Three habits that prevent this:**
1. **One-in-one-out** — every new item that comes in means one old item leaves
2. **10-minute evening reset** — every evening, every surface to its done state
3. **Quarterly mini-clean** — one zone per month, 30 minutes, prevents annual accumulation

The goal is to make the annual spring clean smaller and easier each year as the apartment stays more organized between cleanings.

## Spring Cleaning Priority Order

1. **Bedroom closet seasonal swap** — most impactful, sets the tone
2. **Kitchen pantry and fridge** — declutter and deep clean
3. **Living room full reset** — clear, clean, and refresh
4. **Bathroom** — declutter and deep clean
5. **Donation run** — same day, do not delay

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does spring cleaning take in a small apartment?**
A focused spring clean of a 500–650 sq ft apartment takes one full day if you work consistently, or two half-days across a weekend. Doing one room per evening over the course of a week is also effective if a full day is not possible. The decluttering step adds time but saves it in the cleaning step.

**What should I throw away when spring cleaning my apartment?**
Expired pantry food and medications, expired cosmetics and personal care products, duplicate kitchen tools, broken or non-functioning items, clothing that hasn't been worn in a year or no longer fits, accumulated paper (junk mail, old receipts, outdated paperwork), and anything that "might be useful" but hasn't been useful for over a year.

**How do I do a seasonal clothing swap in a small Canadian apartment?**
Store winter items (heavy coats, wool sweaters, thermals, snow boots) in IKEA SKUBB bags under the bed or in labelled bins on the top closet shelf. Bring spring/summer clothes back into the main closet. Before swapping, apply the three-box test to both sets — spring clean is the best time to donate anything that no longer fits your lifestyle or body.

**What cleaning products should I use for a Canadian apartment spring clean?**
Most surfaces can be cleaned with a simple multi-purpose cleaner or diluted white vinegar. Baking soda paste works for grout and tub scrubbing. For oven cleaning, use the self-clean cycle if available. Non-toxic options are better in a small apartment where ventilation is limited. Method, Attitude (a Quebec brand), or Mrs. Meyer's are available at most Canadian grocery stores.
